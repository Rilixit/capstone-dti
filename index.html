01 — WHAT REFURB MEANSA DEFINITION, A TABLE, NO FINE PRINT

Refurbished ≠ used.

re·fur·bished /riːˈfɜːbɪʃt/ · adj.

A previously owned device restored to full working order through professional inspection, repair and cleaning — then resold with a warranty, and a clear conscience included.

A used laptop is a gamble: you’re buying whatever the last owner left behind, blessed or cursed. A refurbished one has been through a workshop first — inspected part by part, repaired where needed, wiped clean of its previous life, graded honestly and sealed with a warranty.

02 — THE PROCESSAVG. TURNAROUND: 3 DAYS

Six gates between their drawer and your desk.

01
SOURCED
  1. STEP 01
    DAY 0 — INTAKE

    Sourced, serialised, wiped.

    Corporations retire perfectly good machines every 24–36 months. Lease returns, trade-ins, overstocks — they all land on our intake bench, get logged by serial number, and have every drive erased to the NIST 800-88 standard before anything else happens. Your data stays yours; theirs stays gone.

  2. STEP 02
    60–90 MINUTES

    Sixty minutes of suspicion.

    A 30-point checklist leaves nothing to charm: display uniformity, hinges, every port, webcam, speakers, keyboard, trackpad, thermals, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth. Battery health is read straight from the controller — never guessed at.

  3. STEP 03
    AS NEEDED

    Fixed properly, or not shipped.

    Anything below spec gets replaced — batteries under 85% health always are, fitted with new cells. Failed parts go back into our recycling stream, never into a landfill. If we couldn’t fix it, you’ll simply never see it listed.

  4. STEP 04
    20 MINUTES

    Cleaner than your last phone.

    Chassis ultrasonic-cleaned, keyboard de-grimed key by key, screen polished to nothing. The test: it should smell like a new machine and absolutely nothing like its previous owner’s karak.

  5. STEP 05
    HONEST COSMETICS

    Graded by hand, photographed for real.

    A means no visible wear at arm’s length. B means light marks that will never affect a single pixel. C means it has lived — and is priced like it. Every listing shows the actual unit, not a stock photo of “one like it”.

  6. STEP 06
    12-MO WARRANTY

    Sealed with a passport.

    Each machine ships in recycled, plastic-free packaging with its own printed passport: test results, battery health, grade, and a 12-month warranty. Thirty days to change your mind, free return label in the box.

05 — NOTHING WASTEDWHEN REPAIR FAILS, RECYCLING WINS

When a part can't be saved, we don't landfill it.

Not everything makes the cut. A cracked logic board, a swollen battery, a shattered display — sometimes a part is just dead. But "dead" doesn't mean "garbage." We strip every unrepairable unit down to its raw materials and route them to certified e-waste partners. The metals get smelted, the plastics get pelletized, the toxics get neutralized.

LOGIC BOARDS

Smelted for metals.

Gold, silver, copper, and palladium are recovered from dead PCBs. A single ton of circuit boards yields up to 40x more gold than a ton of mined ore — minus the ecological devastation of mining it.

BATTERIES

Hydrometallurgical recovery.

Swollen or end-of-life lithium-ion cells go to specialized recyclers who extract the lithium, cobalt, and nickel. These critical minerals go straight back into the supply chain for new batteries.

SCREENS & GLASS

Crushed and separated.

Shattered glass and digitizers are crushed. The silica is reused in industrial aggregates, while the thin optical films and indium tin oxide are separated out for specialized reprocessing.

CHASSIS & PLASTICS

Sorted by polymer.

ABS, polycarbonate, and magnesium frames are sorted by polymer type, shredded, and melted down. The resulting pellets are reused for non-critical consumer goods and automotive parts.

07 — QUESTIONSTHE HONEST ANSWERS

Asked, answered.

Refurb is still an unfamiliar word to a lot of people, and that’s fair — the industry spent decades blurring it. If something here isn’t covered, ask us directly. The workshop crew answers email between benches, usually within a day.

A refurbished one has been inspected part by part, repaired where needed, professionally cleaned, data-wiped and graded, then resold with a warranty. You’re buying a certified machine, not someone’s history.

They’re cosmetic only — every grade is fully functional. Grade A: no visible wear at arm’s length. Grade B: light marks on the case, screen flawless or near it. Grade C: honest, visible wear — a scratch here, a shiny palm rest there — priced accordingly. Every listing shows photos of the exact unit you’ll get.

Guaranteed at 85% health or better — and if a battery tests below that, we fit brand-new cells before the machine goes on sale. The exact health and cycle count are printed on the passport that ships with your device, so there’s no guessing.

Every drive is erased to the NIST 800-88 standard — the same one governments use — before refurbishment even begins. Drives that can’t be reliably wiped are physically shredded and recycled. Your machine arrives factory-fresh, with nothing aboard but the OS.

Nothing goes in the bin. Dead logic boards are smelted to recover gold and copper; swollen batteries are sent to specialized lithium recyclers; shattered glass and plastics are separated and pelletized for industrial reuse. If a part leaves a machine, it’s because it’s about to become something else.
